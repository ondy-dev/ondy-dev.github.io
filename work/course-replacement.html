Case study 03

A swap-not-delete flow where the hours update live

Role
Designed course editing and replacement end to end: cards, hierarchy, filtering, recommended replacements, the swap flow
Timeline
2026
State
Interactive wireframe, version 5
Tools
Figma · HTML wireframe on the product's tokens

I like flows where the rules stay visible. Here the hour math is on screen the whole time, a locked course tells you why it's locked instead of disappearing, and nothing happens until you confirm. It's a small flow, but it's the kind I think about most: what the screen should say so the person never has to guess.

Problem

Every apprenticeship carries required classroom hours. When a course is cancelled, full, or wrong for the apprentice, a supervisor has to replace it without dropping below the hour requirement, without touching courses the program has locked, and without the apprentice seeing a half-finished plan. They also need to see what else would satisfy the same competency without searching a catalog.

What I did

  • Put course editing inside the training plan's edit mode, as a side panel scoped to one level, so a swap always has its context.
  • Designed the course card: provider, hours, format, included or free, the competencies it covers, and a lock state with its reason ("Can't be removed or swapped"). Locked courses stay visible.
  • Made the swap a replace, not a delete-and-add: pick the course to replace, then choose from "Courses you can use instead", already filtered to the same competency and level, with a recommended option first.
  • Kept the hours math on screen. Nothing changes until "Confirm changes", nothing reaches the apprentice until the plan is published, and Cancel swap returns to the list with no side effects.

Result

The flow lives in the interactive wireframe of the training plan (version 5) with the rest of its edit mode: locked courses visible with their reason, a pre-filtered replacement list, and a running hour total against the requirement.

What I'd change: run the hour math against a real catalog. The wireframe uses five sample courses, and a real one will have edge cases I haven't seen.
