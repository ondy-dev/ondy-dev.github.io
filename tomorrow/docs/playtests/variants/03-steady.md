# Nell, variant 03: "steady" (anxiety 3/10)

Played the real game through the harness, one input at a time. Session file: `scratchpad/harness/sessions/nell-03-steady.txt` (50 inputs).
**Result:** the true ending ("with the truth") on the 7th Tuesday. Two of those seven days were wasted on changes that counted for nothing, so the shortest version of her exact route is 5 Tuesdays.

## The second Tuesday

She saw **no pokes.** She changed the very first moment, 7:14, so none of the `[day two]` lines ever showed. The only push was the morning's "You could do anything today. That's the worst part." For her it isn't the worst part, it's permission. She had a plan from night one ("You should have gone to the tower") and acted on it before the game could test her.

Honest call: this Nell would never resist all seven. The poke day is built for someone who hesitates, and she doesn't. That's fine as design, but it means a whole kind of player never learns the without-knowing ending exists. Nothing later says "you could have just let it go, back then", so she can't regret skipping it.

## Friction

**Inputs that didn't do what she meant**
- `what did lourdes say` and `why didn't you tell me` (day 1) → "You mean to *what did lourdes say*." The `isSpeech` regex in `game.js` `whatYouMeant()` misses question words (what, why, when, how, would, can, did, are…). These should use `meantSay`.
- `text maya can we talk` (day 2) → sends "> hi." and says "before you can stop yourself." Her words got replaced and her decision got recast as an impulse.
- `look at the counter` (day 3 diner) → "You haven't made yourself look." She just did. It reads as a correction, not a nudge.
- `i'm sorry. i'm proud of you` (day 3 driveway) → generic goodbye, "the small things instead of the big ones."
- `go up the tower and ask her if she'd stay` (day 5) → the verbatim tower scene, and not caught as a repeat.
- `would you stay if i asked you to?` (day 6) → "“Stay,” you say. “Please. Stay.”" A question became a plea.
- `text maya` (Wednesday) → "Later." She has waited seven Tuesdays to text her. One word reads like the game closing a door, not restraint.

**Confusing moments**
- Day 3: the identical PEACE_NIGHT after a *different* change. No signal that "goodbye" and "tower" count as the same fix, so the copy just looks like a bug.
- Day 5: the tower rerun repeats the "Why didn't you tell me?" exchange after she has known the answer since day 2.
- THINK at day 6 says outright "There's one thing you haven't tried. Letting every moment go by." For this player that's the whole solution, handed over. It made the last day feel assigned, not discovered.
- Day 7 diner: she never earned `nobody_laughed`, but the clear telling gives it to her ("It's at the TV. It was always at the TV."). DESIGN.md says the honest day shouldn't mention things Nell never found out. That holds for the night, but not for the diner, driveway or street moments.

**Where she nearly quit**
- **Day 5**, after the tower rerun. Two wasted days in three, and the second one ignored what she typed. A real calm-efficient player would probably give it one more day, and if that day had also come back verbatim, she'd stop.

## Proposals

### 1. Ask *if*, not ask: "would you stay?"
- **Moment:** driveway (also the tower, from 3:15)
- **Typed:** `would you stay if i asked you to?`
- **Needs:** `reason` (better: `why_secret`)
- **Learn:** none (or a new soft fact like `would_stay`)
- **Fix:** none. It's a free reply and the moment stays open.
- **Sketch:**
  > “Would you? If I asked?”
  > Maya holds the box tighter. “Don't ask me that, Nellie. Not unless you mean it. Because I'd say yes.”
  > _ (Say it. Or press {enter} to let it happen.)
- **Failed version (no `reason`):** “Would I what? Stay for what, Nell?” She waits for an answer you don't have.
- **Why:** players who ask questions get their question turned into a plea, which steals the choice and makes the keep fix feel like a trick. Letting the question be a free reply means asking for real becomes a decision she makes, so "Like she was waiting for permission to give up" lands on someone who actually gave the permission. (In `words`, catch `would you stay`, `would she stay`, and `if i asked` before `stay`.)

### 2. Knock, and actually talk
- **Moment:** porch
- **Typed:** `knock and say sorry` (with `why_secret` known)
- **Needs:** `why_secret`
- **Learn:** none
- **Fix:** sorry (same fix, a different case)
- **Sketch:**
  > “I know why you didn't tell me,” you say, before she can say it's fine. “You were right. I would have asked.”
  > She sits on the step. You sit one step down, like when you were nine. You talk until her mom honks, and it's the most honest you've been all year.
  > Night: You talked it all the way through. It still happened. Talking about it is just a nicer way of standing in the driveway.
- **Failed version (no `why_secret`):** the current knock scene, unchanged.
- **Why:** her whole personality is "talk it out", and the current scene closes with "Neither of you brings it up again." Letting the talkers talk, and *still* not fixing it, makes "Every fix fails for a different reason" true for the "adult" player too.

### 3. A goodbye that knows where she's going
- **Moment:** driveway
- **Typed:** `i'm sorry. i'm proud of you`
- **Needs:** `reason`
- **Learn:** none
- **Fix:** peace (already the case)
- **Sketch:**
  > “I'm proud of you,” you say. “Chicago's lucky. I mean it.”
  > She sets the box on the hood. “You were the only one who ever thought it was a real thing.” You carry boxes until her mom honks.
  > Night: [from driveway] You were in the driveway this time. You said all the right things. You still feel like you're standing in it.
- **Failed version (no `reason`):** the existing goodbye: "the small things instead of the big ones".
- **Why:** "proud of you" is the obvious adult line, and right now it's swallowed. Also, the same PEACE_NIGHT after a different change reads like a bug. One `[from driveway]` line fixes both.

### 4. The tower, the second time
- **Moment:** 3:15 (tower), or 7:14 `text`, once the other peace route is done
- **Typed:** `go up the tower and ask her if she'd stay`
- **Needs:** `why_secret`
- **Learn:** none (or `feelings`, if she looks at the sketchbook up there)
- **Fix:** none new. Count `tower` and `text` as one change for the repeat check.
- **Sketch:**
  > She looks up before you're off the ladder. “Hey, Nellie.” You already know everything she's about to say. You let her say it anyway.
  > [if the input asks about staying] “Don't,” she says, before you finish. “Up here I'd say yes.”
  > The repeat night: “Why does it sound like you practiced that?”
- **Failed version:** n/a (the tower is never locked).
- **Why:** right now the second tower trip plays verbatim and isn't counted as a repeat, so an efficient player loses a day *and* hears Nell ask a question she knows the answer to. Folding `text`/`tower` into one key in `hasMade` is the smallest fix. A `[replay, if why_secret]` short telling is the nicer one.

- **Typed:** `text maya`
- **Needs:** none
- **Learn:** none

## Takeaway for the author

The calm, efficient player gets out in 7 Tuesdays, and it could be 5, without ever touching the half of the game that's about Nell herself. Her first sensible move, texting Maya, delivers `reason`, `why_secret` and `tried_to_tell` in one scene, which is also the lock on the true ending. After that, the way out is a checklist that THINK prints for her ("There's one thing you haven't tried. Letting every moment go by."). She never reads the message she left on Read, never hears Mom's half-sentence, never looks at the photos or the sketchbook, and never names what she feels. The last night still tells her "It was you", and she half-believes it, because the only part of her own she ever saw was the Dollar General joke. Her friction isn't hidden content. It's that her style of input (questions, deliberate texts, "proud of you", "talk it out") keeps getting translated into a more anxious Nell's actions ("Please. Stay.", "before you can stop yourself", "the small things instead of the big ones"). Two changes would cover most of it. First, let questions be questions: free replies that keep the moment open. Second, think about adding one fact about Nell's own part (`read` or `feelings`) to the lock on the true ending. Then "That's what happened. You were there. It was you." would land on someone who has actually looked.
