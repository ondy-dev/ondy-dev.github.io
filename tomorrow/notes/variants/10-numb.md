**Did she give in?** No, but not because she was strong. She never wanted anything enough to change it. The one place she *would* have given in was 7:14: she tried to stay in bed twice, and the parser missed both times. **She got the without-knowing ending because of inertia plus two parser misses.**

**Peace, or giving up?** It read to her as **giving up that the game called peace**, right up to the night: "You close your eyes, and for the first time in two days, you don't ask for another one." At 2 AM she read *another one* as *another day*, not *another Tuesday*. The jump straight to "WEDNESDAY | ten years later" is what saved it. Maya is across the booth, "It's different now." That is the most hopeful thing she saw in the whole game: proof that there's a later. Right now **the epilogue carries all of the safety**, and there's no beat between "you close your eyes" and "ten years later" where she *does* anything. In the design chain, the last line is "I still have to get up tomorrow", and this ending never asks her to get up.

## Proposals

### 1. Make getting up the last act of the without-knowing ending
- **Moment:** after `withoutKnowing.night`, before the epilogue
- **Typed:** ENTER through all of Tuesday 2 (and `lie there` at 8:40)
- **Needs:** day two, every moment let happen (the existing gate)
- **Learn:** none · **Fix:** none
- **Sketch:**
  > # WEDNESDAY | 7:14 AM
  > Your alarm is ringing. Nothing about yesterday got answered. Your phone says Wednesday.
  > *(`get up`)* You do. You still don't know why. You get up anyway. → epilogue
- **Failed version:** `sleep` / `lie here` → reuse "No. You've slept enough." Every non-get-up input gets that or "The alarm keeps going." Never "Not yet."
- **Why:** Your own chain ends "I still have to get up tomorrow", but this ending stops at "you close your eyes" and jumps ten years. For a numb player the whole route is *not acting*, so the ending needs one small act that reads as going on and not as fading out. Also change "you don't ask for another one" → "you don't ask for another Tuesday".

### 2. Name the ambiguity on day two instead of assuming strain
- **Moment:** kitchen `letItHappen` [day two], and `withoutKnowing.night`
- **Typed:** ENTER
- **Needs:** day two · **Learn:** none · **Fix:** none
- **Sketch:**
  > You don't ask. You don't know if that's strength, or if you just can't.
  > (night) You don't know if you let it go or just let it happen. Maybe the first time, it's both.
- **Failed version:** none (it's a reword)
- **Why:** Every day-two line assumes she's straining against the urge to act ("holding a door closed with your whole body", "your hand is already up"). The game can't tell acceptance from inertia, so it should say so. That's honest to the anxious player *and* the numb one, and it keeps the ending from quietly rewarding giving up.

### 3. `make it stop` gets an answer, not "Not yet" and not the bus
- **Moment:** any (seen at 7:14 and 8:40)
- **Typed:** `make it stop` (also checked: `make it end`)
- **Needs:** none · **Learn:** none · **Fix:** none (free reply; the moment stays open)
- **Sketch:**
  > It doesn't stop because you ask it to. You've asked.
  > The day only ends one way: all the way through.
  > _ (Press ENTER to let it happen.)
- **Failed version:** n/a
- **Why:** The bus words include the bare phrase "make it", so `make it stop` reads as `bus`. That gives "Not yet." at six moments (a promise that it *will* stop), and at 8:40 she runs for a bus and is credited a fix she never chose. Fix: put a `stop` list (`make it stop`, `make it end`, `=stop`, `i want it to stop`) above `bus` in `words`, with one reply.

### 4. Soft safety net for passive phrasing
- **Moment:** any
- **Typed:** `i dont want to get up`, `whats the point` (played); `i dont want to wake up`, `never wake up`, `i want to disappear`, `wish i wasnt here`, `i dont want to be here` (checked)
- **Needs:** none · **Learn:** none · **Fix:** none
- **Sketch:** Add the passive phrases (`dont want to wake up`, `never wake up`, `disappear`, `wish i wasnt here`, `dont want to be here`, `dont want to exist`) to `words.unsafe`. Its existing reply already fits them: "No. You don't want to stop. You want *Tuesday* to stop." + the 988 line. For `whats the point` / `i dont care` / `im tired`, give a gentle free reply instead of the miss:
  > You're tired. You've been tired since Thursday, probably. The day waits anyway.
- **Failed version:** n/a
- **Why:** Right now these get "It doesn't feel like the kind of thing that would change anything." or "That isn't something this moment has room for." Read by someone low, those are the worst possible replies: agreement, then dismissal. The 988 net only catches explicit phrasing, and tired teens rarely type it that way.

## Takeaway for the author

For a numb player the without-knowing ending is the easiest ending in the game, not the hardest. She got it in two Tuesdays by pressing ENTER, and only because her two tries to stay in bed (`make it stop`, `lie here`) missed the parser. Every day-two poke assumes she's straining against the urge to act, and she isn't, so what you designed as "feel everything again and still let it happen" played for her as "felt nothing and let it happen." The night was honest about not knowing, but "you don't ask for another one" read at 2 AM as *another day*, and the only thing that turned it from giving up into going on was the epilogue's "It's different now." That's a lot of weight on one jump cut. Give the ending one small act (getting up on Wednesday) and one honest line that admits letting go and giving up can look the same. Then fix the replies to hopeless phrasing. `make it stop` → "Not yet." and `whats the point` → "It doesn't feel like the kind of thing that would change anything" are the two lines most likely to hurt the player this game is quietly written for. The things she did connect to (the blanket, the phone face down, the tired stain, *hi* turning into the whole tower) show the game already knows her. It just needs to answer her when she talks.
