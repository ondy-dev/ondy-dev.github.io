# Nell, variant 01: "frozen" (anxiety 10/10)

Played on the real game (`story.js` + `game.js`) through the harness, one input at a time. Session file: `scratchpad/harness/sessions/nell-01-frozen.txt` (69 inputs).

**Result:** the true ending ("with the truth") on the 14th Tuesday, the last day in the budget. She **lost the true true ending on the second Tuesday at 7:14** by hiding under the blanket, which was the most frozen thing she did in the whole run. She never found out that door was there.

---

## Who I am

> It's 2 AM and I'm not asleep. I'm lying on top of the covers rereading a thread I've already read. If I don't move, nothing else can go wrong. That's not true, but it feels true, and feeling is louder. Every option looks like the one that makes it worse. So I hold my breath and wait for it to fix itself. It never does. I wait anyway.

| | |
|---|---|
| **Anxiety** | 10/10. Freezes. Every choice looks wrong, so not choosing feels safest. Her chest "does the thing" and she holds her breath instead of breathing. |
| **Fears** | Saying the wrong thing (she already did, and it came out without her deciding). Hearing it said to her face. Things changing. |
| **Hopes** | That it fixes itself. That Maya texts first. That if nobody moves, nobody leaves. |
| **Habits** | Stays in bed. Rereads things. Holds her breath. Looks at old photos instead of new people. |
| **Tells (as a player)** | Presses ENTER a lot. Types two or three words. Uses free actions (LOOK, THINK, *look at the ceiling*) to stall. Types "..." when asked to say something. Repeats whatever the night told her she didn't do. |

There was no explicit poke at 7:14. There didn't need to be. "You could do anything today. / That's the worst part. You could do anything." For a 10/10, that line is the poke.

**Verdict:** she never got close to reaching the second-Tuesday ending by paralysis, because her paralysis didn't look like ENTER. It looked like the blanket. The game *can* tell the difference between hiding and letting it happen (hide is a change, ENTER is acceptance), and thematically that's defensible: hiding isn't living it. But it never *says* so. The hide branch has no `[day two]` lines, and the night is the ordinary hide night. So a frozen player closes the true true door with the most frozen action available, and nothing in the game, then or later, tells her it was a door. The only hint comes on the last card, 12 days later: "There were other ways to spend them."

Read from the code, not played: if she had pressed ENTER at 7:14, her diner habit (`hold my breath` → `breathe`) would probably have ended day two at the diner anyway. And had she resisted in words, `dont ask` at the kitchen matches `talk` → the mom branch (she'd ask), and `dont run` at 8:40 matches `bus` (she'd run). For this kind of player, saying no to a poke can trigger the poke.

## Friction

**Where she nearly quit (and where a real player like her would):**
- **End of Tuesday 4.** She typed `scroll up` because the night before said "You didn't scroll up." The game answered "Your thumb hovers. You don't scroll up." and then "You didn't scroll up." again. She'd gotten the same contradiction two days running, and for an anxious player it reads as *you did it wrong and we won't say how*.
- **End of Tuesday 5. This is where she would really close the tab.** She tried to do nothing, and the night said "you already started pulling at the thread… You can't stop halfway and call it letting go." Then THINK showed only "Maya is leaving tonight. The bus is at 8:45." By then the game had told her that acting was wrong (hiding, scrolling) and that not acting was wrong too, after five Tuesdays with nothing learned. (In the simulation she kept going. A real 10/10 might not come back.)

**Confusions:**
- Tuesday 2 is special, and nothing tells you. Hiding closes it silently.
- "So this time you scroll up. / The last message in the thread isn't yours." The *last* message is at the bottom, so you don't scroll up to it. A teenager will notice.
- The ready hints ("Every one except the first", "There's one thing you haven't tried. Letting every moment go by.") said she'd never done something she did on Tuesday 5. She went ahead anyway, but with dread, not relief.

**Code-read risks for this player type (not played):** `dont ask` (kitchen) → mom branch; `dont run` (8:40) → bus; `dont text her` (7:14) → text. Only `dont knock`, `dont say anything` and `dont go` (3:15) are covered. On day two, a player who says no to a poke in words gets the poke.

## Takeaway for the author

The game *can* tell paralysis from choice, but it does so by state, not by intent, and it never tells the player which one it saw. This Nell pressed ENTER through all seven moments twice: on Tuesday 5 it was hiding ("you already started pulling at the thread"), and on Tuesday 14 the same seven keypresses were letting go. Between them, her most frozen act, hiding under the blanket on the second Tuesday, counted as a *change* and silently closed the without-knowing ending she was arguably made for. So a frozen player doesn't reach the true true ending by paralysis; she misses it by paralysis and never learns it existed. That said, the best writing in the game is written for her. The phone lines ("like it might bite", "You could read it every morning for the rest of your life"), the healed diner ("It still feels like it's at you"), the blanket night, and the feelings admission she reached without leaving bed all landed like they knew her. The cheapest high-value fixes: make the felt phone case honor "reread / scroll up" (that's where she almost quit), give the no-facts ENTER-through night its own true line (that's where she would have quit), add `[day two]` lines to `hide` so the most important distinction in the game gets said out loud, and let her hold her breath and stay silent before the game breathes and speaks for her.
