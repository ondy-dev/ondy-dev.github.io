## Friction

- **7:14 looks are a trap.** `look past the tower at the highway` played the `tower` branch and ended the second Tuesday, the only day the without-knowing ending exists. At 7:14 "look at…" isn't free (game.js `looking && this.momentIndex > 0`), so any look that names a place fires that place. She never found out day two was special.
- **"stay" with a negation.** `I wasn't always going to stay. I wanted out too.` → "“Stay,” you say. “Please. Stay.”" and the `keep` fix. Command.mentions matches whole words with no negation, so "not stay", "wasn't… stay" and "never stay" all beg her to stay. For this player that's the worst possible misread: the game puts her opposite into her mouth, then THINK records it ("You've asked her to stay"), and it counted toward opening the way out.
- **"take me with you" has no home.** Driveway: "That isn't something this moment has room for." Day-two driveway: "It doesn't feel like the kind of thing that would change anything." `let me go with you` gets "Not yet.", which hints at the bus. The miss lines read as verdicts on what she wants.
- **The "say what" nudge contradicts her.** `tell her I want out of Calder too` → "You don't know which of the hundred things to say." She just named which one.
- **Her question gets rewritten.** `Mom, did you ever want to leave Calder?` became "Mom. What did Lourdes say?" `Theo, how much is a ticket on that bus?` got the diner truth and no ticket.
- **The forty dollars is unreachable.** `check the drawer…` and `count my money` both miss. Only `inventory` shows it.
- **No highway anywhere.** The window, the street and LOOK all show the tower. `look at the highway`, `walk out to the highway`, hitchhiking and `pack a bag` all miss. "Tomorrow you'll find a road that actually goes somewhere" promises a road the game doesn't have.
- **The bus repeat is generic.** Maya never speaks on the bus, but the repeat has her ask "Why does it sound like you practiced that?" and the night says "If you'd said it at 6:31." She's the player most likely to ride the bus twice, and her repeat is written for the driveway.
- **Day-one grammar.** "You mean to *where is the bus going*." "You mean to *take me with you*." `whatYouMeant` only detects speech by its first word (i, you, please…), so a question or "take me…" reads as an action.
- **Near-quits:** day 6 (bus repeat: the promised road isn't there), and day 7 (made to say "stay"), where a real player like her would actually quit.

## Takeaway for the author

For a player who wants out, the game has one road: the 8:40 bus, with Maya. The drain is a great image ("You can't get out of a day on a bus"), but it answers whether she can escape the *day*. This player wants to know whether she can leave *Calder*, and whether that wish was ever hers. Every other way she tries to say it fails. "take me with you" hits a miss line that reads like a verdict ("That isn't something this moment has room for"). "I wasn't always going to stay. I wanted out too." gets matched on *stay* and makes her beg Maya to stay, which is the moment a real runner quits. The forty dollars, her whole secret, can't be picked up. The game already knows her, though: the clear porch line, "*Go.* Get out of here. One of us should," is the truest sentence in her run, and she only gets it by giving up. The cheapest fix is words, not branches: route "with you / wanted out / not staying" away from `stay`, make 7:14 looks free (she lost the second Tuesday to `look past the tower at the highway`), and give the drawer one reply. The bigger idea is a `wanted_out` fact found in her own room, so "take me with you" can fail early ("*You?*") and succeed late ("Get your own bus. Promise me you get it.").
