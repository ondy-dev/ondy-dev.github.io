# Nell, from the inside

_A simulation of Nell's inner life through the Tuesday that "Tomorrow" is built on. It's the reference for designing deeper decision trees._

**How to read this.** There are two registers. Blockquotes are Nell: first person, present tense, in her voice. Plain text under **Design notes** is for the author. Names in `code` are facts, branches or moments from `story.js`. **NEW** marks anything the game doesn't have yet. Invented details that would become canon are marked **(proposed canon)** so you can veto them.

**New names this document proposes** (all optional, all specified in section 10):
facts `last_joke`, `outside`, `sideways`, `unfinished`, `already`, `glass`, `road`; one new fix, `answer`; and one engine tag, `[not day two]`. Section 10 lists them in priority order.

## 1. Who I am

> It's 2 AM and I'm counting again. Eleven streets. Three stop signs, one of them bent. Four booths at Dot's, and booth four, which is ours.
>
> I'm Nell. Eleanor if I'm in trouble. Seventeen. I've lived in Calder my whole life, which sounds like a brag until you see Calder.
>
> That's the bit. I do a bit. Something's about to get heavy, I get there first with a joke, and then it can't land on anybody. I'm really good at it. Teachers love me. Mrs. Reyes says I'm a riot. Nobody has ever once called me a good listener, and I've noticed, and I made a joke about it.
>
> The stain on my ceiling is shaped like a state. Maya says Ohio. It isn't Ohio. I've checked all fifty. It isn't any of them. It's just a shape that looks like it ought to have a name.
>
> I don't like it when things move. In ninth grade the Dollar General moved the chips to the other side of the store and I didn't go in for a month. That's a joke. It's also true. Most of my jokes are like that if you hold them up to the light, so I don't hold them up to the light.
>
> When something's wrong, my body knows first. My chest does a thing, like a hand closing around a tennis ball. Mom says breathe in through your nose like you're smelling soup. What soup. Whose soup. It works, which is the most annoying thing about it.
>
> I don't ask for things. Not my jacket back. Not the eleven dollars. If you ask, somebody can say no, and then you know, and you can't un-know it. If you don't ask, everything's still possible. That's not a philosophy. That's just math.
>
> Maya is the only person I don't do the bit with. With her the volume's lower. I don't know how to say it better than that, and I'm not going to try, because trying is how you find out what it is.
>
> There's forty dollars in my desk drawer, for nothing in particular. That's the most honest sentence I own, and it's a lie.
>
> Sometimes a word comes around the corner at me. I cross the street. I'm good at crossing the street. In Calder there are only eleven of them, and I know every one.

### Trait sheet

| | |
|---|---|
| **Fears** | Change, specifically things *moving*: the chips aisle, the empty hook on her door. Being told last, which to her is proof she was never anyone's first person. Being "the one who stays" as a fact about who she is rather than a choice. Asking and hearing no. Being *seen* wanting something, because in an eleven-street town being seen means someone calls your mom. The word. And silence after a real question, which she fills with a joke before it can finish being a question. |
| **Hopes** | That nothing changes. Secretly, that Maya feels the same way and it never has to be said out loud. Smaller: that someone will ask her the exact right question, so she never has to volunteer anything. |
| **The buried dream** | Not a place. A counter somewhere where nobody knows her order. At Dot's, Dot starts her grilled cheese when the bell over the door rings **(proposed canon)**, so Nell has never said her own order out loud in her life. She'd like to, once, to a stranger, and find out whether she'd say the same thing. That's the whole dream. The forty dollars started as *in case* money, in case of what she never finishes, just like Mom's sentence. Lately, without her permission, it has started to sound like *in case I want to*. |
| **Habits** | Jokes instead of listening. Changing the subject. Phone face down. Counting: streets, booths, minutes, dollars. Staring at the stain. Avoiding until it explodes. Never asking for things back. Rehearsing conversations on walks and replaying them at night. Should-statements. Leaving before a thing can finish happening to her (the diner, the street, the driveway). |
| **Tells** | Her chest does "the thing." Her voice goes "flat and strange." She laughs a half-second too early. She sits on her hands. She looks at the jacket instead of Maya's face. She walks in loops. She doesn't remember standing up. She says "fine" when she means "go." |
| **What she'd never say out loud** | "I'm in love with my best friend." The word. "Don't go." "Ask me to stay." "I read it." "I want to leave too." "I'm scared of you being happy somewhere I'm not." |

**Design notes**

- **The loop is her hope come true.** She wanted nothing to change and the feeling to stay true without anyone saying it. That's exactly what a time loop is, and it's hell. The fixes fail partly because each one asks her to change something, and the only version of her hope that can exist is the loop itself. That's worth keeping in mind for the way out: leaving the loop means giving up the hope, not giving up Maya.
- **The stain is the unnamed thing.** "A shape that looks like it ought to have a name" is how Nell treats her feelings and herself. Wednesday's "The stain on the ceiling is just a stain" already pays this off. Section 7 suggests one free reply that bridges the two.
- **The buried dream is kept small on purpose.** She can't want a place, because wanting a place is change. She *can* want five minutes of being unknown. That links the dream to the closet without naming it (being known in Calder means being watched: *"Not on Main Street. Somebody would call your mom."*). It also keeps the station fix wrong in the right way: following Maya's dream isn't having one of her own. She spends her *in case* money on someone else's bus.
- **Two unfinished sentences, one house.** Mom's "Friends who aren't—" and Nell's "in case—" are the same move. So is Nell's own unfinished line on the street (*"She always goes up there when she's—"*). Nell inherited it at that kitchen table.

## 2. The original Tuesday, moment by moment

### 7:14 AM: her room

**What she notices.** The alarm is already ringing; she's been awake since it was dark. The phone face down on the nightstand is the loudest object in the room. The stain. The hook on the door, empty since March. She doesn't look at the corkboard, because she knows where the last strip is without looking. The tower over the Hendersons' roof. Mom's radio doing the weather for somewhere else.

**How anxiety distorts it.** It edits her memory, and the edit is grammatical: *I didn't reply* becomes *she never replied*. As long as the phone stays face down, "something" can still be nothing.

**Her body.** A sore jaw, because she clenches in her sleep. The thing in her chest is a low hum, not the full fist yet. She lies very still, as if stillness could keep the day from noticing her.

> Alarm. Already. I was already awake. I was awake when it was dark and I was awake when it went gray.
>
> Maya never replied last night.
>
> Which is fine. She's probably asleep. She sleeps like a dropped coat.
>
> *for real this time*
>
> No. She never replied. I sent her the stain and she never replied. That's what happened.
>
> The phone's face down. It's been face down since it's been face down. If I don't turn it over, it's a phone. If I turn it over, it's a thing that happened.
>
> "I have to tell you something." That's what people say before they say they're moving, or they're sick, or they hate you, or they found out about you. That's four things, and that's only the ones I thought of in one breath. Anything is the worst thing.
>
> The hook on the door. Still empty. I keep looking at it like the jacket's going to walk back on its own.
>
> Get up. Eggs. Tuesday.

**Almost:** she reaches for the phone and gets her thumb on the edge of the case.
**The brake:** *"If I turn it over, I'll have read it. And then I'll have read it last night too."* She already knows what it says. Turning it over would only admit that.

**Design notes**

- The first lie in the game is the one Nell tells all day: something *she* didn't do becomes something that was done *to her*. *She never replied* is the same move as *everybody knew* and *they were laughing*.
- "Replied" means Nell sent something first, which matches *"The last message in the thread isn't yours."* **(proposed canon)** At about 11:49 Monday she sent Maya a photo of the ceiling stain: *its not ohio*. The joke goes first, so the real thing can't come. Maya's 11:52 answer, *for real this time*, means "not the bit." This gives the phone scene Nell's own message to look at, and it rhymes with Thursday (see chain 6.2, fact `last_joke`).
- **Hidden choices the game doesn't offer yet:**
  - **Call her.** Today "call her" falls through to `talk` and sends her to the tower. Nell would never call. A free reply would cover it: *"You don't call people. Calls are for when somebody's grandma died."*
  - **Turn the phone over.** This already works (it matches `phone`), but it's the most exact verb for her fear. In felt mode the default case could add the brake line above.
  - **Count.** A free reply that fits anywhere (see section 10).
  - **The drawer / the forty dollars.** A free reply: *in case* (see section 10).

### The kitchen

**What she notices.** Cold eggs: Mom made them before her shift and left them under a plate. Mom's back. Mom asking the coffee maker. The pause "long enough to park a car in." "Is all." The keys. The screen door that isn't opening. The fridge photo. Half a piece of toast.

**How anxiety distorts it.** It mind-reads in the wrong direction. She hears *"You and Maya doing anything tonight?"* as a question about *her and Maya*, the thing, not about the move. So she guards the wrong door. The cruel part is that she's half right: Mom's "Friends who aren't—" shows Mom was circling both things.

**Her body.** Heat climbing her neck. The fork speeds up, and her mouth is full on purpose, because you can't be asked a follow-up with your mouth full. Shoulders up. When Mom kisses her head she goes still like a deer.

> The eggs are cold. She made them before her shift and put a plate on top, which is love, in this house. Love is a plate on top of eggs.
>
> "You and Maya doing anything tonight?"
>
> She asked the coffee maker. She asks the coffee maker the hard ones.
>
> Why is that a hard one. Why is "you and Maya" a hard one. What did she hear. What does she think she—
>
> "Nothing," I say, with my mouth full. Nothing, nothing. We never do anything. We sit in booth four, and that isn't anything.
>
> "Lourdes came by the pharmacy yesterday, is all."
>
> Is all. There's no such thing as is all. When Mom says is all, there's a whole other sentence standing behind it in the dark.
>
> Did Mrs. Reyes say something. About us. Did she see— what. There's nothing to see. We repainted a water tower. That's civic.
>
> She's at the screen door. Keys in her hand. Not leaving.
>
> Ask her. Just say: what did Lourdes say.
>
> She kisses the top of my head. She doesn't do that. She hasn't done that since I had my tonsils out.
>
> Okay. So it's bad. Whatever it is, it's tonsils-bad.
>
> I put that in a drawer. I eat the eggs.

**Almost:** "Mom—" It comes out too quiet (this is canon, from `dayOne`).
**The brake:** *"If I ask, she'll have to turn around."* Mom only says the hard things to the coffee maker. A real question would make them look at each other, and neither of them knows how.

**Design notes**

- **The double subtext is the richest thing in this scene.** Mom's question is about the move, and Nell hears it as being about her. Both are true. A line for heal mode once `mom` is known: *"She was asking about the bus. She was also asking about the other thing. You only heard the other thing."*
- **"I thought you knew, baby"** (from the `mom` branch) contradicts the pause and the coffee maker. Nell would catch it on a replay. It works if it's a kind lie, so lean in (section 10, P7).
- **Hidden choices:**
  - **Hug her.** Today `hug` routes to the `mom` branch, which asks what Lourdes said. For Nell, hugging and interrogating are opposite moves.
  - **"Is all what?"**
  - **Finish her sentence**, which only works after `feelings`.
  - **Let her kiss your head on purpose.** This belongs to the last day (see chain 6.6).

### 11:40 AM: Dot's Diner

**What she notices.** Burnt coffee and lemon cleaner. Theo with the pot, which Dot hates. *"The thing where you pretend you're fine."* The word *station*. *Eight forty-five.* Theo's face. The laugh.

**How anxiety distorts it.** Mind-reading (they're laughing at me). Overgeneralizing (everybody knew, the pies knew). Time stretching (Theo's mouth "very far away"). Then a timeline spiral: since *when*? That last one is the first backwards chain she ever runs, and she runs it in real time.

**Her body.** The full fist around the tennis ball. Hearing goes down a hallway. Cold hands, which she sits on. Then her legs decide for her, and she's outside without remembering standing up.

> Theo slides in with the pot, which Dot hates, and pours me coffee I didn't ask for, which is how Theo says hi.
>
> "Are you going to the station tonight, or are you doing the thing where you pretend you're fine?"
>
> What thing. I don't have a thing. "What station."
>
> "Her bus, Nell. Eight forty-five."
>
> Her. There's only one her.
>
> His face does a car crash in slow motion. "She— oh my god. She didn't tell you."
>
> Somebody at the counter laughs.
>
> Okay. Okay okay okay. They know. Of course they know. Dot knows everything. Dot knew about my tonsils before I did.
>
> How long. How long has everybody known. Since Thursday? Since summer? Since March?
>
> March. She's had my jacket since March.
>
> Was I giving her a going-away present, and nobody told me that's what it was?
>
> Theo's still talking. His mouth is at the end of a long hallway. Soup. Smell the soup. There's no soup. There's burnt coffee and lemon cleaner and the whole diner looking at me.
>
> Don't look. If you look and they're looking, it's true. If you don't look, it's only probably true.
>
> I'm outside. When did I stand up.

**Almost:** looking at the counter, and asking Theo *"How long has she known?"*
**The brake:** *"If I look and they're looking, it's true. If I don't look, it's only probably true."* For the question: *"Because he'd tell me."*

**Design notes**

- "Everybody knew" is **partly true**: Mrs. Reyes told the diner at six. The distortion isn't *that* people knew. It's that they were laughing, and that they'd known *for a long time*. Theo's "this morning" undoes more of her pain than the TV does.
- **(proposed canon, optional)** If Maya's acceptance came in March, the jacket started the day she knew. Nell suspects it here. If you make it true, one sketchbook date or one line from Mrs. Reyes would land hard. If you don't, it stays Nell's catastrophizing, which also works.
- **Theo's "She said she tried"**: who is "she"? If it's Maya, then Theo knew more than "found out at 6 AM," and Nell would spiral about Maya telling Theo things before her. See the flags in section 10.
- **Hidden choices:**
  - **"How long has she known?" / "Who else knows?"** Theo answers. This is the 4.8 chain.
  - **Look at the counter.** It's a free reply today (*"You haven't made yourself look"*). It could change once `nobody_laughed` is known.
  - **Go to her house now.** *"I had nine hours."* (6.10)
  - **Bring Theo with you to the driveway**, as a shield. Low priority (section 10).

### 3:15 PM: Main Street

**What she notices.** The Dollar General, twice. The Baptist church. The feed store and the little bus-stop sign beside it, which she has walked past her whole life and never once read **(proposed canon)**. The tower over the roofs. The crooked A.

**How anxiety distorts it.** Rumination becomes a route. She isn't walking anywhere; she's walking *around*. The town becomes a track.

**Her body.** Fast walking with nowhere to go. She can't stop, because stopping means deciding. Her face is set to "normal," because Main Street has eyes.

> I've been walking since the diner. Three and a half hours of walking in a town you can cross in twenty minutes. Do the math. I did.
>
> Dollar General. Church. Feed store. There's a little sign by the feed store with a bus on it. Today I don't read it on purpose.
>
> Dollar General.
>
> I'm practicing what I'll say. "Hey. So. Chicago." No. "Congratulations, I guess." No. "Were you going to tell me, or was I supposed to find out from the bus?" Ooh. That one's good.
>
> It's not good. It's mean. It's good *because* it's mean.
>
> Over the roofs, the tower. CALDER. The second A is crooked because she was laughing so hard she couldn't hold the brush, and I said leave it, and she left it.
>
> She might be up there. She always goes up there when she's—
>
> Don't finish it.
>
> When she's waiting on something.
>
> When she's waiting on me.
>
> If I go up, she'll say it. Out loud, to my face, and then it's hers and it's true. If I don't, it's still only Theo's. A rumor. Rumors can be wrong.
>
> Dollar General.

**Almost:** she turns at the corner where you can see the ladder.
**The brake:** *"If I go up, she'll say it."* As long as Maya hasn't said it herself, it's still a rumor.

**Design notes**

- **The rehearsal is where the driveway line comes from.** *"Or was I supposed to find out from the bus?"* becomes *"When? From the bus?"* Nell already practices conversations, which is why the repeat mechanic's *"Why does it sound like you practiced that?"* cuts so deep. **(proposed canon)** The missing hours from 3:15 to 6:30 are spent on the church steps, rehearsing. Every good line she writes is mean, and she keeps the mean one because it's the only one that doesn't make her cry.
- **Her unfinished sentence mirrors Mom's.** The clear line *"She's been waiting for you since Thursday"* finishes it, which is lovely.
- **Hidden choices:**
  - **Rehearse / practice.** A free reply that shows the speech (section 10).
  - **Read the bus sign.** A free reply. Note that "feed store" is in the `bus` words, so at 3:15 it currently says "Not yet."
  - **Go to her house.** Mrs. Reyes is home and Maya is at the tower (section 10, P13).
  - **Count.**

### 6:30 PM: the Reyeses' driveway

**What she notices.** The trunk "open like a mouth." The box: BOOKS (HEAVY) (SORRY). She reads the SORRY as meant for her. The jacket, sleeves pushed up. Charcoal on Maya's thumb. Maya chewing the inside of her cheek. The streetlight.

**How anxiety distorts it.** The mean laugh. The drift down the driveway. And, most precisely, **the second half of Maya's sentence gets rewritten** (see the design notes).

**Her body.** **(proposed canon)** She stops at the curb and never steps onto the driveway. Arms crossed, hands in her armpits. Her face goes hot, then cold. The rehearsed speech is gone. Her throat closes on the soft things and opens for the sharp ones.

> I had a speech. A whole speech, on the church steps. It's gone. All that's left is the mean line, because the mean line is the only one I said out loud.
>
> Her mom's trunk is open like it's going to eat the house.
>
> She's carrying a box that says BOOKS (HEAVY) (SORRY). Sorry. She wrote sorry on a box. She can say sorry to a box.
>
> She's wearing my jacket.
>
> "Nell."
>
> "Everybody knew." That's not the speech. That's not even the mean line. That's just what was on top. "Theo knew. My *mom* knew."
>
> "I was going to tell you."
>
> "When? From the bus?"
>
> There it is. For one second I'm so proud of that line I could die.
>
> She laughs. It's small, and it's—
>
> "You were always going to stay here, Nell. That's just you. That's fine."
>
> That's just you.
>
> That's the thing I'm scared of, and she said it like the weather. Like Mrs. Reyes at the diner. Like it's a fact about Calder: eleven streets, one water tower, one Nell, who stays.
>
> The streetlight goes off and on, and every time, she's farther away.
>
> "Say something."
>
> I have a hundred things. I can feel all of them. If I say one real one I'll cry in the Reyeses' driveway, and Mrs. Reyes will come out and hug me, and the whole town will know that too by breakfast.
>
> So I pick the one that ends it fastest. That's what I do. The diner, the street, the driveway. I leave before the thing can finish happening to me.
>
> "Fine. Then go."

**Almost:** stepping onto the driveway; taking the box from her (it says HEAVY); "Don't."
**The brake:** *"If I say one real thing, I'll cry, and Mrs. Reyes will hug me, and the whole town will know that too by breakfast."* Being seen is the danger. It's the closet's logic, applied to grief.

**Design notes**

- **The distortion is surgical.** Her memory keeps the half of Maya's sentence that's true and confirms her fear (*"You were always going to stay"*). It replaces the other half. The real second half, *"And if you'd asked, I would have stayed with you,"* puts the power in Nell's hands. The fake one, *"That's just you. That's fine,"* takes the power away and confirms what she already believes about herself. **Her mind picked the version that hurts more and blames her less.** The truth is *worse* for her anxiety than the lie, because it means it was up to her. That's why the fixes multiply after the tower: once she knows she had the power, every fix is her either using it or refusing it.
- **The SORRY on the box**: Maya meant it for whoever had to lift it, and Nell takes it personally. A heal-mode free reply: *"SORRY. For whoever has to lift it. She's always been polite to boxes."*
- **"Fine" is in the `original` words**, so typing "it's fine" plays *Fine. Then go.* That's accidentally perfect, because "fine" *is* how Nell says "go." Keep it.
- **Hidden choices** (all expanded in sections 6 and 10):
  - **Look at her face.** Gated.
  - **Answer Thursday's question.**
  - **Keep the jacket**, or the impossible version, ask for it back.
  - **Ask about Chicago.**
  - **Step off the curb.**
  - **Make a joke.** A free reply.
  - **Say you're proud of her.** That needs `reason` first.

### The porch

**What she notices.** The flinch. The box. The door, closed rather than slammed. The porch light. Maya's shape behind the curtain, not moving away from the door.

**How anxiety distorts it.** All-or-nothing thinking. If she knocks and Maya doesn't open, that's an answer forever. Not knocking keeps it "just a door." Then she hands the decision to an object.

**Her body.** Hand up, hand down. Knuckles an inch from the wood. Locked knees. **(proposed canon)** She stands there for about forty minutes, the same forty minutes she didn't move on the tower in August. It's the same body, once out of love and once out of fear.

> She looks like I hit her. I've never hit anybody. Now I know what it looks like from this side.

> The porch light is on.

> Knock. Two knuckles. It's a door. I've walked through it a thousand times. I've never knocked on it, actually. I just walk in.

> That's how bad it is. I'm on the porch of a house I walk into, thinking about knocking.
>
> If I knock, she opens it, and then I'm standing here with nothing again. I already used my one thing. "Fine. Then go." That was my whole move.
>
> If I knock and she doesn't open it, that's an answer. That's an answer forever.
>

>

**Almost:** knocking.
**The brake:** *"If I knock and she doesn't open it, that's an answer forever. If I don't, it's just a door."*

**Design notes**

- **She outsources the decision to the light.** This is day one's whole stance: letting things decide so it isn't her. The way out has to be the *opposite* move with the *same* action. On day one she doesn't knock because the light decides. On the last day she doesn't knock because she decides. The clear text *"You let it be what it was"* already carries this.
- **(proposed canon)** Maya was holding the switch. A clear-mode line: *"It clicks off. She was the one at the switch. She waited exactly as long as you did."*
- **Hidden choices:**
  - **Text her from the porch**, twenty feet away. It's the safest-distance apology there is, and very Nell. Optional: it could be a `sorry` variant that fails because the phone buzzes on the other side of a door nobody opens.
  - **Walk in without knocking.** "Go inside" and "go in" already route to `knock`. Good.

### 8:40 PM: home

### 11:52 PM: the night

**What she notices.** The time, because she checks the phone for it and then puts it face down *again*. The ceiling. The hook. The highway she can't see.

**Her body.** She can't get warm in her clothes. She flinches at her own "Fine. Then go." every time she replays it, as if it's being said to her.

> 11:52. I look at the phone for the time and put it face down again.
>
> 11:52. Something about that number. I don't look at why.

**Almost:** texting her.
**The brake:** *"She's somewhere in Indiana with her head on a window. If I text her now, it's the first thing she sees in Chicago. I'd have to be the first thing she sees in Chicago. I don't know how to be that."*

**Design notes**

- **Every night in the game is stamped 11:52 PM**, the exact minute Nell read Maya's message on Monday. Intended or not, it's the loop's real wound. The day she keeps reliving began the moment she put the phone down, and the loop starts the morning after. **The two mistakes she'd actually take back, Thursday's joke and Monday's 11:52, both happen before 7:14 Tuesday.** That's the most important structural fact in this document (chains 6.12 and 6.29, fact `outside`).
- The night is ENTER/THINK only, and anything typed at night is passed to the morning. That's fine. The full spiral is in section 3.

## 3. The 11:52 PM replay

The first night, in the order her mind actually goes.

> Somewhere out on the highway the bus is getting smaller. I can feel it getting smaller, like a tooth being pulled very slowly from very far away.
>

**Design notes (the whole day)**

Letting this day go means letting go of a day she believes was *cruel to her*. That's forgiveness without evidence. On day one she didn't do anything because she didn't know she could, and she went numb ("You don't remember standing up"). On day two she knows exactly, feels every one, and chooses. **Every `[day two]` resist line should make her feel more, not less.** That's the rule for auditing the draft. ### Why someone would choose this

All five of these are true at once, and the night shouldn't pick one:

1. **The loop runs on questions.** Every "what if" is fuel. You can't beat rumination by answering it, because each answer breeds two more questions. You can only stop feeding it. This is the author's own reason, and it's the deepest one.
2. **Knowing doesn't un-happen anything.** She lived the day twice without the answer. The answer would be one more replay.
3. **She's seen what she does when she acts.** The person who'd be steering is the one who said "Fine. Then go."
4. **Exhaustion.** Two full days of feeling everything.
5. **Fear of the answer.** Some of this *is* avoidance. Maybe Maya was laughing. Not checking protects her from that. An honest version admits it.

### Peace or exhaustion?

Both. At 11:52 they're indistinguishable from the inside. Peace isn't something she feels. It's something she *decides* while tired. What makes it more than exhaustion:

- **Exhaustion hides, and she didn't.** The `hide` branch is the exhausted version of letting go, and it doesn't count. On day two she was awake for every moment and felt all of it.
- **She claims nothing grand.** The current last line, *"for the first time in two days, you don't ask for another one,"* is exactly the right size. It doesn't say she's at peace. It says she stopped asking. Keep it.
- **It only becomes peace later.** That's what the epilogue is for. At 11:52 it's a decision. Ten years later it turns out to have been the right one. The game shouldn't let it feel like peace *yet*.

**Design notes on the current night text**

- Rings true: *"You still don't know why she didn't tell you." "You don't know if she was laughing." "It happened anyway. It already happened. You were there."* (that last one echoes the true ending's *"You were there. It was you."*).
- **Add the possibility of cowardice.** Without it, the ending risks reading as pure wisdom, and Nell isn't purely wise. One line: *"Maybe you're just scared of the answer. You let it go anyway."*
- **Add the phone, unrevealed.** *"Your phone is face down. You don't know what's under it. You leave it."* It tells a first-time player there was something they never saw, without telling them what, which is replay bait and true to her.
- **"You probably won't" vs. the epilogue.** The night says she'll probably never know. The epilogue shows she does, ten years later. That's fine as dramatic irony, and it fits the author's *"you get them by going forward."* But if the whole game is older Nell telling it, she's saying "you probably won't" while knowing she did. Read it as her telling it the way it *felt*, which is consistent with the narrator who lies.
- **What she never found out**, for reference. On this path, these stay unknown inside the story: the text and Read, Thursday, the sketchbook, the real driveway sentence, the TV, Theo's 6 AM, Mom's sentence. The night names two of them. Naming more would turn it into a list. Two, plus the phone line, is right.

## 7. Living the fixes

This is the order a real Nell would try things in. Each attempt is chosen by the previous night's logic. She starts with whatever exposes her least and works toward whatever exposes her most. Players won't follow it exactly, but each night's last line should point the way she would go next.

### Day 2: the second Tuesday, and where she breaks (sorry)

She holds through the phone, the kitchen, the diner, the street. She even says "Fine. Then go." again. Then, on the porch, her hand is already up.

> I made it through the whole day. I let Theo say it. I let them laugh. I walked past the tower. I said it, on purpose.
>
> And then my hand was up, and I didn't put it there, and it was coming down on the door.
>
> She opens it before it lands. She was right there. The whole time.
>
> "I didn't mean it."
>
> "I know you didn't."
>
> "I meant some of it."
>
> "I know that too."
>
> Okay. Okay. We're carrying boxes. I know which ones are heavy because she wrote it on them. Her mom honks at eight, and Maya hugs me on the porch like nothing happened.

**The turn:**

> Like nothing happened.
>
> We're doing the bit. Both of us. She's doing the bit *with me*. She's never done the bit with me.

**The next morning:**

> I lasted until the porch. I almost had it. Then my own hand.
>
> And now I've pulled the thread. I can feel it hanging off the day.
>
> Sorry fixed the part where I said it. It didn't fix that I said it. If I'd just been decent in the driveway, there'd be no door to knock on.
>
> Okay. Don't say it, then. Say goodbye. Be normal. I can be nice for one driveway.

**Design notes:** Knocking is the first fix for Nell even off the day-two path. It needs no knowledge, and it lets her keep the fight and apologize afterward, which is the lowest exposure available. What breaks it is that Maya, the one person Nell doesn't perform with, performs *for* her: "like nothing happened" is Maya being polite. It's the first time Nell sees her own defense from the outside. If the player gives in on day two, a single `[day two]` line in the branch text should say *where* she broke (P15).

## 8. The way out (with the truth)

> 1. If I'd said goodbye: I did. It was good the way a photograph is good.
> 2. If I'd knocked: I did. It happened nicer. It still happened.
> 3. If I'd asked her to stay: she stayed, and stopped drawing.
> 4. If I'd answered her: five days late.
> 5. If I'd told her: I did. She said it back. She still got on the bus.
> 6. If I'd gone with her: the road went around.
> 7. Every version ends at 8:52. The bus is the one thing in Calder that has never once been different. Even late is the same late.
> 8. Every fix was me trying to *feel* different at 11:52. Not to make it different. To make me different, at 11:52.
> 9. The things I'd really take back are Thursday and Monday. They aren't in here.
> 10. So Tuesday isn't where I went wrong. It's where it showed.
> 11. And she didn't tell me because I was the one who could stop her. Because I mattered most.
> 12. So the day is what it is because of who we both were. Not because I stood a foot to the left.
> 13. What's left?
> 14. Letting it be what it was.
> 15. Not like the first day. The first day I let it happen because I was scared. I let the light decide.
> 16. This time I'm deciding.
> 17. Which means I'm going to say it. "Fine. Then go." On purpose. Knowing her eyes are red.
> 18. That's the part I can't make okay. I hurt her. Every version where I didn't was one I made up afterward.
> 19. I'm going to say it anyway, because it's what I said.
> 20. And some part of me means *go*. Get out of here. One of us should.

### Why she can't get there earlier

- **Anxiety doesn't take arguments, only evidence.** She can't be *told* the perfect version doesn't exist. She has to watch each one fail. One failure is bad luck, two is a coincidence, three is the day. `FIXES_NEEDED = 3` is exactly right for her.
- **She needs `why_secret`.** Without it, the day reads as rejection (*she didn't tell me, so I didn't matter*), and you can't accept a story where you're the injured party. You keep litigating it. With it, the day is two people afraid of the same thing, and there's no verdict left to win. The current gate matches her logic.
- **She needs to see that the real mistakes are out of reach.** That's what turns "I haven't found the right fix" into "there isn't one in here." The gate doesn't require it, and shouldn't, but it should surface on the ready mornings or in the final night (`outside`, P6).
- **Why the day-two window closes, in her logic.** Once she's pulled one thread and the day changed, her brain has *learned that pulling works*. You can't unlearn that. After day two, the only way out is to pull every thread until pulling stops working. That's the psychological basis for the author's rule. It isn't about knowledge (some day-two give-ins teach nothing). It's about the habit.

### Living it on purpose (the last day, from inside)

- **7:14.** *Maya texted last night. I read it. I'm not going to fix that.*
- **Kitchen.** She lets Mom kiss her head, and notices it. *She can't fix it either. This is what she does instead.*
- **Diner.** *It's at the TV.* She remembers standing up.
- **Street.** The hardest one. She lets Maya wait. *She's been waiting since Thursday. I'm letting her keep waiting.* The clear line, *"You let yourself not go,"* is exactly right.
- **Driveway.** She hears the real sentence, and says hers.
- **Porch.** She doesn't knock, and this time it's her, not the light.
- **Home.** She hears the bus. She doesn't count.

### What isn't neat (and shouldn't be)

- She hurts Maya again, on purpose. Accepting means re-enacting her worst moment while knowing exactly what it does. The final night's *"You hurt her. You wish you hadn't."* is the right register. Don't soften it.
- She's still a little angry at Maya (chain 6.22). Both things are true.
- She still hasn't said the word. The feeling has a name inside her. She doesn't, out loud, about herself.
- She's still in Calder. The forty dollars is still in the drawer.
- She'll still replay it. *"You don't play it back. You play it back a little."* Overthinking doesn't end. It stops being in charge.
- **Acceptance isn't resignation.** *"Chicago was a stupid idea anyway"* (the keep night) is resignation, and Nell puts it in Maya's mouth. Acceptance is the opposite: *Chicago was a good idea, and it cost me her.*
- On Wednesday the phone says *"Later."* She'll text, but not to fix anything. The game is right to stop before she does.

### The two ways out, compared

| | Without knowing (the second Tuesday) | With the truth |
|---|---|---|
| What she lets go of | the felt version: mocked, laughed at, last to know | the true version: loved, and hurt anyway |
| Who she has to forgive | Maya, as she remembers her | herself |
| What it costs | not knowing, maybe forever | knowing, and doing it anyway |
| Kind of letting go | before the questions are answered: part faith, part exhaustion | after they're answered: understanding |
| How like her it is | the least. It's the one thing an overthinker can barely do, which is why it's only possible before the habit of pulling forms. | the most. It's the natural anxious route: see everything, then let go. |

**Final-night additions** (use sparingly, at most two): `[if outside] Thursday and Monday were never in here.` and `[if already] The jacket's on a bus somewhere. It was always hers.`

## 9. The epilogue, from older Nell

_Ten years later, a Wednesday, booth four. Only on the without-knowing path._

> I'm twenty-seven, in booth four, and I've just finished telling it.
>
> I tell it in "you." I always have. *You wake up. You go to the diner. You say it.* If it's "you," I can watch her from across the room. If it's "I," I'm back in the driveway.
>
> I told it the way it felt, not the way it was. I told Maya she laughed at me. I watched her sit there and listen to herself being mean in my version, and she let me finish.
>
> That's new. At seventeen I didn't let anybody finish.
>
> The girl at the register didn't know my order. I had to say it out loud, to a stranger, in Dot's. Grilled cheese. It turns out I'd have said the same thing. I just wanted to be the one who said it.
>
> I lived that day twice, that night. The second time I didn't change anything. I still don't know if that was brave or just tired. Ten years, and I've decided it doesn't have to be one or the other.

### How she tells it now

- **The second person is distance.** That's how she survives the telling, and it's why the reveal works: the game's "you" was never the player's. It was Nell holding her seventeen-year-old self at arm's length. The first "I" in the epilogue is her stepping back into it.
- **She tells it *felt*, to the one person who can correct it.** On this path the narration never heals: Maya laughs, the diner stares. So the whole game is Nell telling Maya a version of the day where Maya was cruel, and trusting her to hear it. That's the opposite of performing, and it's the real ending. The narrator who lies was always waiting for Maya to say *"I wasn't laughing."*
- **What she admits:** the Thursday joke. Reading the text at 11:52 and turning the phone over. "Fine. Then go." And that part of her meant *go*. That she tells it in "you." That the two Tuesdays were one night of replaying.
- **What Maya admits:** months before Thursday, not just Thursday. The sketchbook instead of her face. Watching it say Read. Crying, not laughing.

### Notes on the current draft

**Rings true. Keep:**
- *"I always tell it like it happened to somebody else."*
- The charcoal on her thumb, ten years on. She never stopped drawing, which is the quiet opposite of the keep fix.
- *"Months before Thursday."* It also supports the jacket-since-March reading.
- *"Somebody at the counter laughs at the TV."*
- *"Same booth. Same smell. It's different now."*

**Rings false, or too tidy:**
1. **Maya volunteers "I wasn't laughing."** It's truer if Nell *asks*. Nell never asks for things, so a twenty-seven-year-old Nell asking the question she couldn't ask at seventeen is the growth, made visible in one line.
2. **"I know. I mean, I know now."** On this path she couldn't know until Maya tells her. If Nell asks, her reply can be *"Okay."* That's the word she wished she'd said in the driveway (section 3: *"Okay is right there."*).
3. **Nell's confession is a clean list.** Older Nell would still put a bit in it, and then drop the bit. Grown-up Nell is still funny, she just doesn't hide behind it.
4. **The "why" is missing, and that's right, but go one step further.** The truth path's answer is crisp (*"if you'd asked, I'd have stayed"*). Older Maya's should be messier, with both fears, because she's had ten years to find out it wasn't one thing.
5. **Everything gets answered.** That pulls against the thesis (*"you never really figure out what happened"*). **Keep one thing unknown:** the porch light. The thing Nell replayed for ten years isn't something Maya even remembers. That's how overthinking actually ends.
6. **Maya takes her hand.** Keep it, but give Nell the smallest possible move: she turns her hand over. For someone who never asks, that is asking.
