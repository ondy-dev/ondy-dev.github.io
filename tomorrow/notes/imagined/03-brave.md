# Brave Nell

_An imagined playthrough of **Tomorrow**, for the design exercise. Nell number 03: the one who says the hard thing to everybody, not just Maya._

**How to read this.** Game prose is in blockquotes, in the game's voice (second person, present tense). **Game now** says what the real game does with that input today: either an existing branch or free reply from `story.js`, or what the play script printed when I typed it (on day two, after a first day of all ENTERs). **NEW** marks a proposal. **(proposed canon)** marks an invented fact you can veto.

**New names this file proposes.** Facts: `the_word`, `out_mom`, `whole_world`, `mom_tuesday`, `theo_too`, `march`, `other_side`, `delivered`. Branches: `mom truth`, `mom tuesday`, `ride`, `theo truth`, `everybody`, `what she was like`, `wrong reyes`, `fight`, `door`, `jacket`, `say go`, `delivered`, `best tuesday`, `church`. One action: `out`. Optional new fixes: `right`, `back`, `reword`.

## 1. Who this Nell is

She's the same Nell. Seventeen, eleven streets, does a bit whenever something gets heavy, has never said the word *gay* about herself, not even in her head.

What changes her is the third Tuesday, when she works out the rule nobody told her: **nobody remembers.** Mom, Theo, Mrs. Reyes and Maya all wake up at 7:14 knowing nothing. So everything is free. Tuesday turns into a rehearsal room for every sentence she has swallowed in seventeen years. She comes out to Mom. She finishes Mom's sentence for her. She answers Theo's question honestly. She asks Mrs. Reyes the question nobody asks a mother. She stands up in Dot's and says it to the room.

> Nobody remembers. That's the trick. I could tell Mom. I could tell the coffee maker. I could tell Dot and the truckers and the pie case. By 7:14 tomorrow it'll be like I never opened my mouth.
>
> So why are my hands shaking?

Her bravery is real, but it's **loop-bravery**: she's brave because it costs nothing. Her endings test that in two ways:

1. **She's brave with everybody in Calder except the one person it's for.** Every hard sentence she says to Mom, Theo or Mrs. Reyes is also a sentence she isn't saying to Maya. The game can name this out loud, once: *"You've gotten very good at saying things to everyone but her."*
2. **Nothing she says sticks.** The loop can hold a confession for one day. Her endings keep running into the same wall: a truth told on a Tuesday has to be told again on a Wednesday before it counts. That gives the game a new reason to let go, one the other fixes don't give. It isn't only that fixing the past fails. The people who'd change *with* you only exist in tomorrow.

**What she wants:** to be known by somebody before the day ends.
**What she's afraid of:** that being known in Calder means being talked about. Mom counts the whole town's pills. Lourdes can't hold anything. The Baptist church sits on Main Street. *"Not on Main Street. Somebody would call your mom."*
**Her tell:** the hard sentence comes out fine. The second half never does. She's her mother's daughter.

## 3. Her chains

Written the way the author builds them: one "but" per line, backwards from the thing she lies awake wishing.

**C1. The word**
> I should have told Mom. At least then one person in our house would have said a true thing out loud.
> But I couldn't have. I didn't have a word for it. I never let it have a name.
> I'd have had one if I'd let myself look. *(`feelings`: the sketchbook, or the fourth frame plus August)*
> But even then. She was already at the door with her keys.
> She wasn't leaving, though. She hadn't opened the door. She was waiting for me to ask. *(free reply `look at mom`)*
> And if I'd asked what Lourdes said, she'd have started a sentence she couldn't finish. *(`mom`)*
> So I finish it for her.

**C2. Everybody knew about me**
> Everybody knew. Theo knew. My *mom* knew.
> But they knew about Maya. That's different.
> Is it? Mom stopped in the middle of a sentence. Dot said *go do something about it.* Theo didn't even ask who I meant.
> They knew about me too.
> Everybody knew about me except me.
> I was the last one to find out about me. And I'm mad that Maya didn't tell me *her* thing?

**C3. Theo**
> I should have told Theo. He asked me straight out if I was pretending I was fine.
> But Theo talks. Everybody in this town talks.
> Theo doesn't, though. He found out at six and held it all morning. He dreaded the booth. *(`nobody_laughed`)*
> So he can keep a thing. So maybe he's keeping something.
> I'd only find out if I went first.

**C4. March**
> Why didn't I ask Mrs. Reyes what Maya was like when she found out?
> Because then I'd know, and then it would be real.
> It's already real. I've seen the ticket. *(`reason`)*
> So ask the next thing. When? What did she say first?
> *March.* She's been wearing my jacket since March.
> She knew the whole summer I was talking about the crooked A.
> I can't go back to March. The loop doesn't go back that far.

**C5. Making them look**
> I should have just said it. Stood up in Dot's and said it to everybody. Then nobody would ever know anything before me again.
> But then Mom would have heard it from Dot instead of from me.
> And Maya would have heard it from her mom.
> And I'd be standing in a driveway saying *I was going to tell you.*
> Which is what she said.
> Oh.

**C6. The fight**
> I should have told her it hurt. Not *everybody knew*. Just: it hurt.
> But then she'd tell me what I did. Thursday. The joke. *(`tried_to_tell`)*
> Good. Let her. I'd rather fight about the true thing than win the wrong one.
> But what if we both say the true thing and she still goes?
> Then at least it was a real fight.
> Is a real fight better than a fake goodbye? I'd have to have one to know.

**C7. The door**
> I should have said it at the door. She was right behind it. *(free reply `window`: "She isn’t moving away from the door.")*
> But if I knocked, she'd open it and see my face.
> So say it through the door. She'd hear. She was right there.
> But then I'd never know if she heard.
> I don't know anything else either. Why would this be different?

**C8. The other way to say go**
> I should never have said *Then go.*
> But part of me meant it the other way. Go. Get out. One of us should. *(the porch's `[clear]` line)*
> She'd have stayed if I'd asked. *(`why_secret`)*
> So *go* was the only thing I said all day that let her leave.
> So I run to the bus and say it again, the right way.
> But it meant both. It still means both.

**C9. It doesn't count**
> I said it. To Mom. To Theo. To the whole diner.
> But tomorrow nobody remembers. It's 7:14 and Mom's at the screen door and she doesn't know.
> So I say it again.
> And again. Every morning. Forever.
> But it will never once be *said*. Not in a day that stays.
> The only day where it counts is the one after this one.
> And I can only get there by letting this one go.

**C10. Mom's Tuesday**
> Why does Mom talk to the coffee maker?
> Because she doesn't know how to look at me and say things.
> But she wasn't always like that. In my baby pictures she's laughing with her mouth open.
> Something shut. I'd only find out what if I asked her a real question. Not *what did Lourdes say.* A real one.
> *Mom. Did you ever have a Maya?*

**C11. The best possible Tuesday**
> If I go up the tower *and* tell her *and* say goodbye *and* don't fight, the whole day's fixed.
> But I only get one change.
> Not if the change is going up early. Then the whole afternoon is mine.
> And I say all of it, up where it's quiet.
> And she says it back.
> And she still has a ticket.

**C12. Delivered**
> Monday night she texted me and I left it on Read. *(`read`)*
> I should have answered.
> But what would I have said? I didn't know what she was going to tell me.
